Predictive alarming

A warning before a blood pressure crash happens — not a monitor that only reacts once it already has.

The problem

Intraoperative monitors are built to alarm at a threshold — they tell a clinician a patient has crashed, not that one is coming. By the time the alarm fires, the team is already reacting to a crisis instead of preventing one. Intraoperative hypotension is common, consequential, and almost always visible in the data minutes before it's visible on the monitor.

How it works

1

Streams live monitor data

Reads continuously from existing intraoperative monitors — blood pressure, heart rate, and related vitals already being captured in the OR.

2

Forecasts forward in time

A sequence model trained on real surgical cases projects where a patient's blood pressure is heading, not just where it's been.

3

Surfaces a warning window

Flags a likely hypotensive event with meaningful lead time, giving the care team room to intervene before a crash rather than after.

Who it's for

Built for every intraoperative phase of surgery where blood pressure instability carries risk — particularly longer procedures and higher-risk patients, where a few extra minutes of warning changes what the care team can do about it.
